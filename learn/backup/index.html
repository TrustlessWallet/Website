Back up your wallet and Terminal

Keep the recovery phrase and your merchant records recoverable when a phone is lost or replaced.

On this page

Protect the wallet keys

The wallet recovery phrase backs up its signing keys. Use Backup wallet in the wallet options, write the words in the exact order and complete the verification. Store the phrase privately and separately from the phone. Never enter it into a website or send it to support.

Merchants also need a separate encrypted Terminal backup. The recovery phrase alone cannot reconstruct the local sales journal, original prices, catalog or receipt settings.

Save your merchant records

  1. As the owner of the active wallet, open Terminal Settings → Backup → Create.
  2. Choose and confirm a backup password of at least 12 characters. Keep the password accessible to you separately from the file. It cannot be recovered through Trustless.
  3. Create the backup and save the encrypted file through the share sheet to a location you can access if the phone is lost. Make fresh backups after new sales, refunds or catalog changes.

A Sales CSV is useful for accounting. The encrypted Terminal backup is the file the app accepts for a restore.

Move to another device

  1. Recover the original wallet using its phrase, or select that wallet if already present. Use the same network as the backup.
  2. Before taking new sales or adding catalog items, open Terminal Settings → Backup → Restore, choose the file and unlock it with its password.
  3. Review the preview and confirm the restore. The destination must have an empty history and catalog. If the backup includes receipt settings, templates or reports, those destination settings must also be empty.
  4. Check the restored sales and items before retiring the old device. Refunds on restored sales stay disabled because the backup might predate a later refund.

Common questions

Will buying Pro restore my sales on another phone?

Store purchase restoration restores eligible subscription access. Recover the wallet with its phrase and the local business records with the encrypted Terminal backup.

What if I lose the backup password?

If the original Terminal still holds the records, create a fresh backup with a new password and keep it safe. The old encrypted file still requires its original password.
