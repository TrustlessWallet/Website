Pay with Lightning

Understand the two balances, pay an invoice and know what to check when a payment takes longer.

On this page

Choose a balance

Your on-chain balance consists of Bitcoin outputs controlled by your keys. Trustless also offers a Lightning spending balance through the Breez SDK and Spark. A shop presenting a Lightning invoice expects payment through Lightning. A regular Bitcoin address is for an on-chain transfer.

Switch to Lightning and use its top-up flow when you want to move bitcoin into the spending balance. Review the quoted terms and fees before sending. Withdrawing to an on-chain address is a separate operation. Normal payments and withdrawals depend on the service being available.

Pay at a counter

  1. Ask the merchant to generate an invoice for your order.
  2. Scan it from the Lightning wallet. Review the amount and fee. When the amount is fixed, it should match the request you agreed to pay.
  3. Confirm once. Check your outgoing payment and let the merchant check their own Terminal. If the result is unclear, compare the two records before attempting another payment.

Receive over Lightning

In the Lightning wallet, open Receive. An amount-specific BOLT11 invoice requests one payment. Share the generated invoice or display its QR code. A Lightning address, when available in Receive, is a reusable address people can use to request payment details.

An invoice can expire. If no payment was made, the recipient can generate a fresh invoice. If the sender has already attempted the payment, first check its status in both wallets.

Common questions

Does a customer need Trustless to pay a Trustless merchant?

The Terminal presents a Lightning invoice. A customer can use a compatible funded Lightning wallet that supports the invoice.

Can I use the Lightning QR in my on-chain withdrawal screen?

Choose a withdrawal or payment method that explicitly supports Lightning invoices. A screen that accepts only Bitcoin addresses expects an on-chain destination.
